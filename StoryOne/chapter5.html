One Eye, One Girl

Currently reading: Chapter 5

Previous Happenings:

After the science lesson, Iris decides to head to the history classroom to get her and her friends' history project to work on... by adding what she had learnt in science.

It doesn’t take me long to arrive at the history classroom, I knock on the door and hear the teacher say “Come in!” So I do just that. Opening the door and shutting it behind me, after it shuts, I walk over to the teacher’s desk. Miss Seddon looks surprised when she looks up from a pile of papers and says “Iris, you shouldn’t really be here at this time, it is free time after all.” I knew I shouldn’t really be here at this time, you weren’t really allowed in the classrooms during free time, unless you were doing revision (which was optional, unless the teacher thought you were doing really badly).

But, the main reason why I was here was to see if I can get the project, so I say “I know… but I have something to ask you relating to the history project which we started earlier today.”

“Ok… but make it quick,” She responds.

And then I ask, “Ok… so, you know how we started the history project earlier today?”

“Yes, I was the one who set the work after all,”

“Well, I was wondering if I could take the one me, Willow and Beatrice were working on up to my dormitory to work on during the rest of free time,”

Miss Seddon thought about this for a while, before she replied with “I’m not too sure, this is only really supposed to be completed during lesson time, to ensure everyone has an equal amount of time to complete it. That should go for any other school work really, unless it’s homework…” I think she then mutters something under her breath about having to allow everyone else to complete it during free time, she then tells me “I guess if you are desperate to aim for those top grades, then I guess I can let you take it, but have you told Beatrice and Willow that you are doing this?”

Truth be told, no I hadn’t, and I wasn’t actually planning on telling them. Then again, they probably wouldn’t want me writing a load of facts and taking up valuable space on the A3 sheet of paper, it is a group project after all. So, I tell the teacher “They will once I’m allowed to take the project”

The teacher looks down at the pieces of paper on her desk, it looks to be some worksheets, from a previous lesson perhaps? and then says “I wouldn’t usually let students do this, but since this work counts towards your end of year grade, I guess I will let you take it, do you know where I’ve kept them?” I shake my head in response, then Miss Seddon points the pencil she is holding to one of the light wooden cupboards and replies with “They’re on the top shelf of that cupboard, if you can’t reach it, let me know.”

I walk over to the cupboard and as I open it, I look towards the upper shelf and see some pieces of paper stacked on the shelf, I assume that this must be the projects. So, I just about manage to reach the top shelf and pull all of the sheets of paper out of the cupboard, they almost all fall down around me, but I steady it so that doesn’t happen, thankfully. I place the projects on a nearby table and begin to look through them to find ours. As I do this, the teacher says “Beatrice and Willow should have put their names on their sheet of paper, if you are working with them, then you should do the same”

Looking through the different projects, I see all of the hard work that has been spilled onto the various pages, admittedly some look better than others. It doesn’t take too long to find the piece of paper which looks familiar and turning the piece of paper, I see Beatrice and Willow’s names written on the back of it. I place it to one side and place the other projects back into the cupboard.

I take the project and begin to head to the door, as I do this, Miss Seddon says “Wait a second, Iris.” I stop and look back and she says “I am ok with you taking the project, but make sure the other two know what you are going to add.”

I respond with “Don’t worry, I will because it is supposed to be a group project after all.”

I begin to open the door, but the teacher quickly adds, “Also, make sure you don’t work too late into the night and especially not during lights out because if you do and you mention my name, I’ll get into a bit of trouble,” I thank the teacher before stepping out into the corridor.

=====

After placing the project in our dormitory, I noticed Willow wasn’t there, so I decide to head to Beatrice’s dormitory first, to tell her what I am going to add to the project. Then, I suppose, I’ll have to wait to talk to Willow about it.

When I arrive at the eighth dormitory (Beatrice’s Dormitory), I knock on the door and wait a few moments for a response. When none arrives, I knock once more and await an answer. Eventually, the door creaks open and I don’t see Beatrice at the door, instead I see someone else, “Oh, hello Iris, I wasn’t expecting to see you here… what did you want?” The person who had answered the door was the person Beatrice shared the dormitory with, her name was Amber.

“Oh, I was wondering where Beatrice was” I say “do you know where she could be?”

Amber thinks about this for a few moments before she replies with “Not exactly, but I think I remember her telling me she had some homework she needed to do, so she could be in the library… if she isn’t there, then I have no idea where she could be.”

“Ok thanks… I’ll go and check there now” I say and walk off in the direction of the library.

It doesn’t take too long to arrive at the library, mainly because it’s relatively near the dormitories. When I do, I open the door and step onto the plush grey carpet, I see a few students sat at the tables scattered across the room, some of the bookshelves cover up the view of the tables, which would probably explain why I can’t see Beatrice here, if she is here. I look around at the dark wooden bookshelves, dark wood seems to be used a fair bit in Hazelwood now that I think about it, the bookshelves don’t appear to be positioned in any order, they, just like the tables, appear to be scattered throughout the library. I look around the library in the hopes that I would find Beatrice.

If it wasn’t for all the hundreds, potentially even thousands, of books in the library, I probably would have found Beatrice a bit quicker than I did. But I eventually found Beatrice and Willow sat at a table, Beatrice seemed to be looking down at some of assignment. Willow must have seen me walking over because she said something to Beatrice, who then looked up and saw me. I took a seat at the table and asked them what they were up to. Beatrice said she was doing some geography homework (due for tomorrow). I guessed Willow was trying to help Beatrice with her homework since she was quite good at geography.

As Beatrice was writing down an answer to a question, Willow asked what I was doing in the library, so I told them both about the project and how I had planned to add all of the information we had learnt in the science lesson to it.

Once I tell them that I had the project in my dormitory, Beatrice asks “Were you even allowed to take that from the classroom? In fact, are you even allowed to go into the classrooms in free time? I thought you could only do that for revision…”

“Well… I don’t think you are, but I went to our history classroom anyway, and the teacher was still there. I asked her if I could take the project and she said I could, all I would have to do is return it for our next lesson.”

After this small conversation, I decide to ask again, for clarity, whether or not I would be allowed to write about what we had learnt in science. Willow looks to Beatrice, and after some thought, Beatrice looks to me and Willow as she says “I guess you may as well, because I don’t think we’re going to get anything else from those books in the classroom anyway…” Then she asks again whether or not we’re actually allowed to add to the project now. I answer the question by saying “Yes… the teacher said we could, but I think that because I have taken it to complete now, the rest of the class will be able to do the same.”

“Hmm… alright then, but you’ll have to try to use as little space as possible to write anything down. The teacher told us that we would only be allowed one piece of A3 paper, so we should try to conserve as much space as possible.”

“Why is that?” I ask, because using only one piece of paper had been mentioned quite a bit now, but I had no idea why…

“To be honest, I don’t know” Beatrice replies, “I suppose it’s so that you are cautious about what you write on there, making sure it’s correct… Unless it’s a tactic done by the teachers to use less paper? I don’t really know…”

It was strange that there wasn’t a definitive answer to this, but from the way Beatrice was hinting towards not taking up a load of space on the piece of paper, I started to wonder if there would be any place in particular I would need to write down these facts, so I ask “Do you want me to write these facts on any particular place on the paper” Beatrice goes silent for a minute, perhaps trying to visualise what it looked like, it might have been easier if I had bought it here.

All she ends up saying is “I’m not really bothered where you place them. Although, maybe it would be better if you placed the facts near the corner of the page…”

“Ok, near one of the corners, got it” I acknowledge. I would definitely remember that as I didn’t want to mess it up. I then turn to look around at the library, at all the different books and begin to wonder whether any information could be hiding in any of them. After all, Beatrice had said that near to no information would be found in the books from the classroom, maybe here might have some of the answers.

Since we had about half an hour left of free time before it would be dinner time, I decided that my time would be better spent checking the various bookcases to see if I could find a book containing any relevant information for the project at hand.

So, I stood up from where I was sitting and began to wander around the library, looking at the different bookcases as I walked. It didn’t take long before I realised that all the bookcases had a piece of yellow paper stuck to the edge of the top shelf, which indicated the category of books on the shelf. For instance, the one I was looking at had ‘fiction’ written on it. Looking around the library, I saw that the yellow papers stuck to the bookcases all seemed to have different words or phrases to encapsulate the prevalent themes of each book on the shelves.

I headed back to the table to write down some of the categories I could remember. All I had to hand at the minute was my timetable, so I see if there was any other piece of paper I could use, and to my surprise, out of the corner of my eye, I see a small paper holder with a note above it saying: ‘spare paper, only take one if you need it (don’t use it to make any paper aeroplanes)’. There wasn’t many piece of paper left in there, so I take one, which appears to be an old English worksheet, but flipping it over reveals a blank page, which will do for now.

I return to the table and, after asking Willow for a pen, write down the categories for the bookcases I had been to so far. During this, Beatrice asked what I was doing, I told her that I was writing down the categories of bookshelves. Which was true, but wasn’t the whole story.

=====

In the end, I was quite surprised at how little was on the list, maybe there was potentially less bookcases than I had initially thought. Anyway, the list looked like this:

  • Mysteries of History
  • Fiction
  • Short Stories
  • Adventure Stories
  • Teen Fiction
  • Fantasy Fiction
  • Non – Fiction

I did have to walk around the library to get the final few categories, but even so, it took up about ten minutes, which meant twenty minutes remained.

I looked down at the list for a while, before asking “What times does the library open?” I figured that once I knew this, I would be able to head to the library in the morning to try to see if I could find any books containing information which might help with the project.

Since Beatrice was focussing on her homework, Willow responds with “I think it’s open from seven in the morning to eight at night… But I don’t think it opens on a Wednesday or a Friday.” Which, thinking about it, today was Monday, I would be able to look tomorrow, and on Thursday and the weekend. I was sure it would be open all (school) year round, but we only had a month for this project, so I didn’t think that would matter.

I made a mental note of this before going back to checking the list of categories I had written. I didn’t see much point in checking the bookcases now, so I decide to cross off the bookcases off which probably wouldn’t have any books relating to the topic we were researching.

I managed to narrow it down to only two categories which may have the information we were looking for: ‘Mysteries of History’ and maybe ‘Non - Fiction’ (depending on whether it actually happened or not)

It seemed as though time was counting down quicker than I expected because it didn’t take all too long before there was five minutes left of free time. I looked up from the list, I was mainly doodling on the piece of paper for the remainder of the time, perhaps I could of checked some of the bookcases now… Beatrice had folded the geography homework and placed it in her blazer pocket. She asked whether we should head down to the main hall. Me and Willow both said different alterations of ‘yes’, so that’s what we did.

On the way out of the library, I did notice another bookcase which I hadn’t seen, I must have missed it… which was labelled ‘geography’, looking at the spines of the books, it seemed to feature books on various wildlife and there looked to be a few maps there as well.

=====

Even if it was lights out, I still had my lamp on, hoping I wouldn’t be caught, and I was adding what I could remember from the science lesson. Some of it was still in my mind, so I decided to release it onto the page.

It didn’t take long before my eye began to feel heavy. So I left it for now, and decided to come back to it tomorrow.
