One Eye, One Girl

Currently reading: Chapter 4

Previous Happenings:

Iris, Beatrice and Willow had just had a rather intriguing lesson and it might even help with the history project the three of them were working on

After the science lesson was over, everyone left the classroom headed down to the main hall for lunch time.

Well… most people set off, but the teacher wanted the bullies to stay behind, because of how late they were, and me, because….well, I wasn’t really sure. We all remained sat down as everyone left the classroom. Once everyone had left, the teacher called me up to her desk, I stood up and traipsed over there, I asked why the teacher had kept me, and she quietly told me (probably so the Evie and her friends wouldn’t hear) “I noticed some people were muttering under their breath and even rolling their eyes when you answered one of my questions” I could tell she looked at Evie at that moment, but she turned back to me as she added “So, if there is anything that is bothering you, then the door to this classroom is open most of the time”

I thought about it, the only thing starting to really irritate me was the fact that Evie, Phoebe and Maisie seemed to keep bullying me, although, I guess they are known as the school bullies for a reason. I just say “Thank you” to the teacher because at least there would be someone I could talk to, well… someone other than my friends, however, for now, I just decide to keep my thoughts to myself.

As I turned to leave the classroom, Evie whispers from the corner of the classroom “Even if you tell the teachers, it won’t stop anything” I guess she must have heard what the teacher told me, and besides, I was kind of expecting that, because there would always be at least one person in this school who hated me (and there was near to nothing I could do about that).

As I walked out of the classroom, I saw Beatrice and Willow standing by the wall of the corridor, everyone else had gone, but they had decided to stay, “You didn’t have to wait for me” I say

“Well… We thought we may as well, and besides” she lowers her voice as she says “we wouldn’t want any more fights, would we?”

“No… But that was completely out of my control, what was I supposed to do?”

“Let’s not worry about that now,” Beatrice decides, for the better, “Anyway, why were you told to stay behind?” I simply told Beatrice that the teacher just wanted to speak with me.

On the way down to the main hall, we were talking about what eye colours we had and what that meant for our personalities.

As I had previously discovered, Beatrice had a brown and a blue eye, that meant she was supposed to be lucky, I asked her if she felt lucky, but she said “To be honest, not really, but maybe I am… who knows?”

On the way, I also realised that Willow had blue eyes, which I probably would never of known had she not told me, her eyes were covered up by her hair most of the time, When I told my friends that I had green eyes, Beatrice asked if I felt jealous of anything, she also asked if I felt if I had bad luck, like the teacher said. I thought about it, what happened at break time and all the bullying I’ve had to endure and the cause of it, in the end, I say “I guess it would be good to have two eyes, that way I wouldn’t be bullied, and I suppose I am fairly unlucky because of that…”

I then began to wonder if there was any way to gain a second eye. I discarded this thought after a while because I was sure it was impossible. Because you most certainly couldn’t go into a shop and ask the shop keeper that you would like to purchase another eye.

=====

When I open the doors to the main hall, I notice how it is slightly busier than it was at break time (which was usually the case at lunch time). We did have to push past some people to get to the back of the hall, which was there the door was, which led to the field.

As we walked onto the field, Beatrice suggested that we should run to the back right corner of the field (I guessed it would make a difference from walking there nearly every time). I agreed to run, but Willow said she would rather walk than run.

So, me and Beatrice stopped walking and I started to count down

“three… two… one… Go!”

We both began to run, at quite some speed, alongside the dark green fence. I knew Beatrice did cross country running as an extra curricular activity, so it was no surprise to see that she was already ahead of me.

As I was running, my mind turned to what happened at break time, I took a glance at where it had all happened, the bullies’ insults and their laughs echoing in my head and how my reflection stared back at me with blood all over its face.

As I was thinking about all of this, I felt as though I was starting to run faster, I could tell I was catching Beatrice, whose long, light blonde hair blew with the breeze, but I knew there was not going to be enough distance for me to catch up with my friend.

A few moments later, I see Beatrice stop running. She must have made it to the dark green metal fence on the other side of the field. I lost, although technically we had never agreed it was a race.

When I reached her, I saw her sitting down on the grass, breathing heavily, and I was the same. I fell to the floor, exhausted, we were both so out of breath.

A few seconds later (which kind of felt like minutes… maybe it was minutes later), Willow arrives at where we were lying down on the grass and she quietly said “You two were both quite quick”

 

I then sit up from where I was lying down and, after taking a couple of breaths, I ask Beatrice “How on earth do you run so quickly?”

“I don’t know… I guess practise makes you better at the things you practise at...” Which I guess is true.

=====

We spent most of lunch time talking about some school stuff, although the conversation eventually turned, thanks to me, to the history project. I decided to tell my friends about how we might be able to use some of the information we had learnt in our previous science lesson for our history project.

To my surprise, Beatrice responds with “I think we could, because it would be information on what makes everyone different, it tells you how everyone has different personality traits, and it happened about 70 years ago. So, we could potentially add it to the project”

“Wouldn’t we have to make sure that it actually happened?” Willow asks.

“What do you mean?” I ask.

“The teacher did say it was never confirmed whether it happened, it could just be a myth and we don’t know…” Willow says.

“I always thought it to be true” Beatrice says under her breath. Then she says to us “I guess it could just be a myth, but we have no idea whether this is the case. Also, didn’t the teacher say that people had been researching it?”

“Yes… but that doesn’t mean that it happened…”

“He can’t just be researching nothing though….” I hear Beatrice say under her breath.

“Here’s a suggestion,” I say, “We could, maybe, not get too angry over this”

After I had said this, Beatrice takes a deep breath before saying “Ok, fine…”

Quite a few minutes pass and lunch time eventually comes to an end, meaning that it was time for another lesson. I checked my timetable and it looked to be home economics, which certainly wasn’t the worst subject, but it also wasn’t the best (in my eye), I remember making a dish and Beatrice refused to eat it, complaining about how she was probably going to get a food poisoning from it, I didn’t blame her, but it didn’t really raise my confidence in my cooking skills.

I then put my timetable back in my blazer pocket and started to traipse to Home Economics.

=====

Soon, home economics was over and that signalled the last lesson we had today, so that meant that it was free time for pretty much the rest of the day. Since it was free time, I decided to head to my dormitory because there would be no school bullies there to make this Monday even worse.

I am secretly glad I wasn’t at Hazelwood last year, because I would have had to share a dormitory with the entire year (of which there is only around ten people), or at least that’s what the first years have to do, thankfully we’re in the second year now, so we don’t have to do that, instead we only have to share a dormitory with only one person.

When I arrived at dormitory number four (they had numbered all the dormitories, somehow I just ended up with one of the unlucky ones), I opened the door and took off my pale orange school blazer and threw it onto my bed, I then sat at the end of my bed and stared at the opposite side of the dormitory where another bed sat parallel to mine, that bed belonged to Willow, who I shared the dormitory with.

As I sat on my bed, I thought about the science lesson and how the group of sorcerers and sorceresses had managed to change our personality traits. I was still trying to get my head around how they could have possibly managed to do it. What Willow said, about it being a myth, could be true, part of me was hoping it wasn’t… Because I wanted there to be a way to end all of the bullying I have had to put up with all this time.

My mind then turns to the history lesson, I then remembered what I asked my friends at lunch time. I wondered whether some of the information would be useful for the project, especially considering the topic we had chosen for the assignment. There could be a chance that we were able to add the information we learnt in today’s science lesson to the project.

The science lesson, if what we were taught about had actually happened, could have actually told us how our personality traits had been decided. I guessed this would help with the project, and our science teacher told us that it happened sometime during the early 1900s and considering it was the early 2000s now, I consider that to be history.

In the end, I decide rather than to be sat in my dormitory for the hour of free time, I should try and get the project from the history teacher, so I can try to work on it during the rest of free time, which, looking at the clock, seems to still be fifty minutes. So, I quickly put on my pale orange school blazer and head down to the history classroom.
